1. WHAT THE THERMAL INFLUENCE MATRIX REPRESENTS
  [MATH] K_is = P_is / P_ss with P = (L_g + H)^-1 is the steady-state normalised temperature of node i when node s is held at the source
         temperature. Column s is a complete equilibrium temperature field; row i collects node i's response to every possible source.
         0 <= K_is <= 1, K_ss = 1. Because P is symmetric, K_ij / K_ji = P_ii / P_jj exactly (verified, max relative error 3.7e-15); the asymmetry is a source-normalisation artefact.
  [PHYS] K_is says how much of a source's temperature excess survives at node i after leaking away along the way. Mean off-diagonal influence = 0.0004.
         Heat conduction itself is perfectly reciprocal; 88% of sampled pairs differ by >10% between K_ij and K_ji only because hub-like nodes (small P_ss) are 'stiffer' sources than weakly coupled ones.

  [MATH] No — influence is clearly non-uniform. Coefficient of variation of C_in = 0.54, Gini = 0.30, Moran's I (k=8 nearest neighbours) = 0.93 (strong positive spatial autocorrelation means neighbouring nodes have similar C_in).

7. EFFECT OF ENVIRONMENTAL HEAT LOSS (beta)
  [MATH] From beta = 0.0001 to 1: mean influence 0.0147 -> 1.21e-05; reach fraction 0.07 -> 0.000; CV of C_in 0.31 -> 2.64; periphery/core ratio 0.59 -> 0.78; Moran's I 1.00 -> 0.45.
         Rank stability: Spearman rho of C_in versus beta_main ranges from 0.53 to 1; top-1% overlap falls to 0.10.
         As beta -> 0 the solution converges to a uniform temperature over the source's component (verified; exact beta = 0 gives a spread of 2.7e-09 K), and (1 - theta) scales linearly with beta.
  [PHYS] Weak loss lets heat equalise across the whole network (position hardly matters); strong loss confines influence to a few segments, so centrality tracks local conductance.

8. GEOMETRY VERSUS TOPOLOGY
  [MATH] Degree and mean incident segment length are coupled (rho = +0.11); degree and centrality of position are coupled (rho with distance to hull = +0.12), so topological and geometric descriptors are not independent.
         Decay of theta is better described along the road network (mean R^2 of ln theta = 0.84) than in straight lines (0.78); median detour ratios 1.15, 1.18, 1.28 (high, mid, low reach sources).
  [PHYS] Heat follows roads: along-road distance explains the decay better than straight-line distance, so Euclidean proximity matters only insofar as it implies short, well-connected road paths.
         Variance split above: geometry-only R^2 = 0.55 vs topology-only R^2 = 0.20, so geometry carries more explanatory power here (the two are correlated, so this is not a causal split).

CAVEATS
  * Conductances are relative (g = 1/L, identical material and cross-section); leakage is uniform; results are not calibrated physical quantities.
  * Spearman correlations describe association, not causation; betweenness may be sampled (see run_config.json) for large networks.
  * Based on the OSM network as downloaded: tagging errors, missing links, bridges mapped as intersections and the chosen network type affect the result.
