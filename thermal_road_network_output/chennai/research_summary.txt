1. WHAT THE THERMAL INFLUENCE MATRIX REPRESENTS
  [MATH] K_is = P_is / P_ss with P = (L_g + H)^-1 is the steady-state normalised temperature of node i when node s is held at the source
         temperature. Column s is a complete equilibrium temperature field; row i collects node i's response to every possible source.
         0 <= K_is <= 1, K_ss = 1. Because P is symmetric, K_ij / K_ji = P_ii / P_jj exactly (verified, max relative error 4.2e-15); the asymmetry is a source-normalisation artefact.
  [PHYS] K_is says how much of a source's temperature excess survives at node i after leaking away along the way. Mean off-diagonal influence = 0.0008.
         Heat conduction itself is perfectly reciprocal; 86% of sampled pairs differ by >10% between K_ij and K_ji only because hub-like nodes (small P_ss) are 'stiffer' sources than weakly coupled ones.

  [MATH] No — influence is clearly non-uniform. Coefficient of variation of C_in = 0.36, Gini = 0.20, Moran's I (k=8 nearest neighbours) = 0.94 (strong positive spatial autocorrelation means neighbouring nodes have similar C_in).

7. EFFECT OF ENVIRONMENTAL HEAT LOSS (beta)
  [MATH] From beta = 0.0001 to 1: mean influence 0.0316 -> 2.8e-05; reach fraction 0.16 -> 0.000; CV of C_in 0.20 -> 1.72; periphery/core ratio 0.66 -> 0.83; Moran's I 1.00 -> 0.44.
         Rank stability: Spearman rho of C_in versus beta_main ranges from 0.40 to 1; top-1% overlap falls to 0.05.
         As beta -> 0 the solution converges to a uniform temperature over the source's component (verified; exact beta = 0 gives a spread of 4.6e-09 K), and (1 - theta) scales linearly with beta.
  [PHYS] Weak loss lets heat equalise across the whole network (position hardly matters); strong loss confines influence to a few segments, so centrality tracks local conductance.

8. GEOMETRY VERSUS TOPOLOGY
  [MATH] Degree and mean incident segment length are coupled (rho = +0.15); degree and centrality of position are coupled (rho with distance to hull = +0.03), so topological and geometric descriptors are not independent.
         Decay of theta is better described along the road network (mean R^2 of ln theta = 0.95) than in straight lines (0.74); median detour ratios 1.26, 1.19, 1.39 (high, mid, low reach sources).
  [PHYS] Heat follows roads: along-road distance explains the decay better than straight-line distance, so Euclidean proximity matters only insofar as it implies short, well-connected road paths.
         Variance split above: geometry-only R^2 = 0.13 vs topology-only R^2 = 0.11, so geometry carries more explanatory power here (the two are correlated, so this is not a causal split).
